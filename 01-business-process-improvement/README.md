# UK Banking Complaints Performance & Process Improvement

**Project Status:** Completed — Independent Business Analyst Portfolio Case Study  
**Industry:** UK Financial Services (Banking)  
**Reporting Period:** January–June 2026 (FCA 2026 H1)

## Project Overview

This project examines banking and credit-card complaints reported by 48 selected UK financial firms using publicly available Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) data.

The objective was to analyse differences in complaint volumes, three-day closure percentages and upheld rates, then apply Business Analysis techniques to explore potential process improvement opportunities.

The project combines **Excel analysis, Power BI visualisation, stakeholder analysis, process mapping, business requirements and recommendations**.

## Key Findings

- **703,339 complaints opened** across 48 selected firms.
- **86% of complaints** in the selected sample were reported by Retail / Commercial Banks.
- **Average three-day closure rates** ranged from approximately 33% to 57% across analyst-defined firm categories.
- **Complaint volume and three-day closure performance** showed a weak positive correlation (Pearson r ≈ +0.183) among the 47 firms with available closure percentages.
- **Complaint upheld rates** varied between categories, highlighting areas for further investigation.

The findings are exploratory and do not establish the internal causes of complaint-handling delays.

## Business Analysis Approach

1. Prepared and analysed publicly available FCA complaints data in Excel.
2. Identified illustrative stakeholders and potential complaint-handling bottlenecks.
3. Developed an illustrative AS-IS complaints-handling process.
4. Defined five KPIs and three proposed business requirements.
5. Created a Requirements Traceability Matrix and proposed TO-BE process.
6. Completed a gap analysis and developed three business recommendations.
7. Built a two-page interactive Power BI report.

## Proposed Business Recommendations

**1. Improve complaint assignment:** Introduce defined assignment rules and queue monitoring.

**2. Improve investigation tracking:** Track evidence requests, outstanding actions and complaint deadlines.

**3. Strengthen complaint registration:** Introduce information validation and structured follow-up.

These are proposed improvements for a hypothetical complaints operation. They have not been implemented or tested within a real financial institution.

## Data Source and Limitations

**Source:** [Financial Conduct Authority — Complaints Data 2026 H1](https://www.fca.org.uk/data/complaints-data-2026-h1)

The analysis covers 48 selected firms, not the full UK financial services market. Firm categories were defined by the analyst. Average percentages are unweighted firm-level averages, and complaint volumes are not adjusted for firm size.

The publicly available FCA dataset does not contain the internal operational information needed to identify confirmed process bottlenecks or evaluate proposed improvements.
